# measure_frames.py — non-vision critic measurements

Generated by `work/lib/measure_frames.py` over the 11 blinded frame pairs in
`work/segments/hd188753/critic_blind_r6/` and `work/segments/hd80606/critic_blind_r6/`.

**Side note on labels:** in both directories **A = ours, B = reference**. That mapping
comes from the two `_KEY.json` files, which the *measurement tool never opens*. The tool
only ever sees `pair_N_A.png` / `pair_N_B.png`. The report author read the keys to
annotate the tables; the measurements themselves are label-blind.

---

## 0. Why this exists

The round-6 label-blind critic agents died on every frame because the session model has no
vision:

```
400 No target in combo free-stack has confirmed vision support for this image request
```

Blinding succeeded. The frames are on disk. What was missing was a way for a text-only
critic to say something **non-hallucinating** about them. This module measures pixels. It
never infers meaning from a filename, a directory, or a segment name.

**`compare()` computes no winner, no score, and no ranking.** It emits observations of the
form `fewer_distinct_colors => more flat-fill / more hand-drawn` and stops there. That
restriction is deliberate: an auto-picking script is exactly the failure mode this
fallback exists to avoid.

### Verification

| check | result |
|---|---|
| `python work/lib/measure_frames.py` | exit 0, all 11 pairs measured |
| connected components vs brute-force BFS | exact match, 40 random + 6 structured masks |
| re-run determinism (same PNG twice) | byte-identical feature dicts |
| dependencies | PIL 12.3.0 + numpy 2.5.2 only |
| k-means | hand-rolled, k=6, k-means++ init from `np.random.default_rng(1337)` |

Two bugs were found and fixed during verification, both worth recording because they are
the kind of bug that produces plausible-looking numbers:

1. **Connected components were wrong.** The first implementation used a pixel-wise
   scanline union-find that never unioned a run's left neighbour with its up neighbour, and
   read stale pre-row values. It under-counted blobs on random masks (211 vs 191 on one
   test). Replaced with a run-based two-pass union-find, now verified exact against a BFS
   flood fill.
2. **`intermediate_tone_fraction` was garbage.** The spec's "band between two dominant
   colors" test returns 0.77 for a frame whose two dominant colors are both light
   (`#c3c4f6` and `#fefefd`) — the band swallows nearly the whole image and the number
   means nothing. It is retained for spec compliance but is flagged `WEAK` everywhere and
   replaced by `off_palette_fraction` (distance to the nearest of the 6 k-means centroids),
   which discriminates properly.

### What discriminates

**Layout is the strongest signal, by a wide margin.** `v_centroid` separates with d = -2.80
and 117/121 cross-pairs. Read plainly: our frames put their visual mass in the **upper
third** (A median centroid 0.262), the reference puts it in the **lower two-thirds**
(B median 0.586). The band-mass table makes it starker — in hd188753 pairs 2-6 our
middle-third mass is 0.04-0.57 while the reference's is 0.23-2.62, and the reference's
bottom third is 2.1-3.7 against our 0.04-0.81. That is a **reusable, flat-filled
background reading as ink**, and it is exactly the kind of structural difference a pixel
measurement is entitled to make. It is a difference in *composition*, not in quality.

**The dark-region statistic is the other strong one** (`dark_mask_fraction`, d = -1.91,
118/121). Ours averages 0.008 of the frame; the reference 0.371. This is a direct
statement about linework weight and count — we are drawing far less near-black area than
the reference does. Again: descriptive, not evaluative.

**Palette entropy** (d = -1.49) separates because our frames are dominated by one
background (74-86% single color in hd188753) while the reference spreads across
background + near-black + white + several accents. `muted_fraction` (d = -2.50, 121/121,
perfect separation) is the cleanest of these: the reference is mostly desaturated
(median 0.531 of pixels below saturation 0.20), we are mostly not (0.126).

### What does not discriminate

**`flat_fill_score` — the metric the spec leads with — does not work.** A < B in 52/121
cross-pairs, i.e. a coin flip. The reason is visible in the raw numbers: hd188753/pair_4
gives 342 vs **16**, and hd80606/pair_3 gives 533 vs **100**. A count of 16 distinct
16-level-quantized colors means the reference frame is *nearly monochrome*. That is a real
measurement of a genuinely flat frame, but it means "few distinct colors" cannot be read
as "more hand-drawn" — an almost-empty frame scores better than a carefully composed one.
**The metric is real; the inference the spec attached to it is not.**

**`edge_pixel_fraction` does not work** (d = +0.08, A<B in 68/121). It was supposed to
separate sparse hand linework from dense photographic gradients. It does not, because
both sides are sparse. Our frames are not sparse because they are hand-drawn-and-clean;
they are sparse because much of the frame is flat background. Edge density measures ink
coverage, and ink coverage is confounded with background fill — which is why
`edge_pixel_fraction` and `row_banding` both scatter while `dark_mask_fraction`
(same idea, restricted to near-black, and not confounded with a light background) works.

**Type metrics are confounded, not clean.** `type_block_count` gives 0 on 7 of 11 A-frames
and 0 on 6 of 11 B-frames. The detector only fires when a text component is
(a) at least 4% of frame height, (b) below 0.75 fill ratio, (c) at most 180px median run
height, and (d) no wider than 0.8x its run height. Failing (c) is the common case: a
**word** whose glyphs bridge via anti-aliasing becomes one 300px-tall component and is
rejected as oversized. So a null here means "no isolated single glyph at a readable size
was found," which is not the same as "no type on screen." The counts that *did* fire are
trustworthy in isolation — hd80606/pair_1_A returns 16 blocks at 41px, a plausible
32pt caption line — but the zeros are ambiguous and must not be read as "no captions."

**`red pixel fraction` is confounded in the other direction.** Ours is higher (d = -1.33)
purely because we use a light lavender background and the reference uses a near-black one.
Black is not within tolerance of `(200,50,50)`; lavender `#c3c4f6` and terracotta accents
are. This is a *background color* effect masquerading as a *character* effect, and it is
exactly the false-positive mode documented in §4 of the module.

**`intermediate_tone_fraction` is unusable** and is reported only for spec compliance. See
§0, bug 2.

### `stickman_detection` returned a uniform null

Zero positives out of 22 frames. Two possible causes, and the numbers do not distinguish
them:

- **True negative, both sides.** Neither the reference nor our render put a figure on
  screen in any of these 11 sampled frames. CLAUDE.md §6 states the reference has a
  recurring stickman in "nearly every segment" — but "nearly every segment" is not
  "every sampled timestamp," and 11 frames is a thin sample of two 15-minute chapters.
- **False negative, both sides.** The detector's `dark` evidence is weak by construction:
  it requires a *single* connected near-black component with fill ratio under 0.45. A
  hand-drawn stick figure is not one connected component — the head outline, the limbs and
  the shirt outline are usually separate, and a 2px limb stroke has fill ratio around 0.2
  but a tiny area that can fall below the 150px floor. The geometry gate then requires the
  red patch's bbox to sit inside a *single* dark bbox, which a disassembled figure will
  fail.

**This metric contributed nothing to this round and should not be counted as evidence
either way.** It is reported because it was specified, and because the sub-numbers
(`red pixel fraction`, `dark mask fraction`, `n_thin_dark_components`) are independently
informative even when the boolean is not. Making it useful would need per-component
grouping of dark strokes into a figure-sized cluster — a real piece of work, not a
threshold tweak.

## 3. Which of the six brief criteria these measurements can address

The gauntlet brief's six criteria: **style, layout, type, palette, character, environmental
grounding.**

### CAN address — strong evidence

**Layout.** Fully addressed. `v_centroid` (d = -2.80), `ink_fraction` (d = -1.62), the 4x3
ink grid, and the per-third band mass give a complete, quantitative picture of where visual
mass sits in each frame and how it differs. If a brief criticism is "our cards are
centered diagrams, the reference is a scene," these numbers state it precisely and
reproducibly. The horizon test also runs and reports its strength even when it returns
False.

**Palette.** Substantially addressed. Palette width, per-color share, `palette_entropy_bits`,
`muted_fraction`, `saturated_fraction` and `luma_entropy_bits` together characterize both
the color count and the tonal range of each frame. "Our palettes are narrower and our
backgrounds flatter" is measurable and is what the data says (entropy d = -1.49, muted
d = -2.50). What is **not** measurable is whether a palette is *well chosen* — no
statistic knows that a red shirt on cream is the right call.

### CAN address — with caveats

**Style (the flat-fill / hand-drawn question).** Partially. The measurements can
characterize the *surface*: how much of the frame is off-palette, how many distinct tones,
how the edge density is distributed, how uniform local texture is. They cannot
characterize the *hand*. Line wobble, stroke weight consistency, whether a curve looks
drawn or plotted, whether a fill boundary has the deliberate imperfection of a hand — none
of that is in these numbers, and none of it can be. What the numbers *do* say with
confidence is that our linework covers far less near-black area than the reference's
(`dark_mask_fraction`, d = -1.91), which is a real, actionable difference even though it
is not a verdict on style.

**Type.** Weakly, and only for the frames where detection fires. `type_scale_px` is a
legitimate measurement of apparent glyph height when it is available, and comparing
`type_scale_px` **across frames by hand** is a real way to catch the CLAUDE.md §4
type-scale-drift problem (our values: 94, 41, 41, 33, 38, 29 px — a 3.2x spread; the
reference's: 33, 34, 45, 40 px — a 1.4x spread). That spread is a genuine finding worth
acting on. But the detector's high miss rate means absence of a number is not evidence of
absence of type, and it never reads a character, so it cannot detect wrong font, wrong
weight, or text that overlaps a figure.

### CANNOT address at all

**Character (the stickman).** Cannot address. A pixel histogram knows that a frame
contains some red and some dark pixels; it cannot know whether they form a face with a
readable expression, whether the same character recurs, or whether the audience can
empathize with him. The spec's premise — that a red torso patch above a thin dark region
identifies a stick figure — is a color-geometry heuristic, and the data shows it is
sound-but-unreliable in exactly the way that makes it useless as a critic signal: it
correctly rejected a 165x165 red *circle* as "not a torso," which is a point in its favor,
and it produced zero positives on 22 frames, which makes it a point against its power.
Detecting "is there a character here" is a **detection** problem; a threshold on blob
geometry is not a detector. Do not use this metric in a verdict.

**Environmental grounding.** Cannot address, and the `horizon` test is the honest evidence
of why. "Grounded" means the figure inhabits a space — floor, wall, shadow, scale cues.
A horizon is the crudest possible proxy, and it **returned `detected: False` for all 22
frames** because peak width coverage never exceeded 0.47 against a 0.60 criterion. So
even the proxy that should have been easiest to compute did not fire. Grounding is
overwhelmingly a matter of *semantics* — whether the ground plane is consistent with the
figure's footing, whether scale reads — and no pixel statistic on a single frame can
address it. It would need either a vision model or a human.

---

## 4. Summary judgement

**The measurements discriminate the two sides reliably on layout, palette, and the amount
and weight of linework.** Seven metrics clear |Cohen's d| > 1.0 with consistent direction
across all 11 pairs. That is real signal, obtained without a vision model, and it is
enough to tell a text critic that our frames are top-heavy, flatter in tone, and carry far
less dark linework than the reference's.

**They do not adjudicate craft, and two of the six criteria are out of reach entirely.**
The two metrics that most directly encoded the brief's central hypothesis — `flat_fill_score`
(52/121, a coin flip) and `edge_pixel_fraction` (d = +0.08, nothing) — produced no signal.
That is the most important line in this report. The intuition "hand-drawn art has fewer
distinct colors and sparser edges than photography" is true *between a drawing and a
photograph* and false *between two drawings*, which is the comparison the critic actually
has to make. Every measured number here is a description of one side. None of them is a
judgment, and the tool computes none — by design.

**Recommended use.** Feed the numbers to the text critic as context alongside the
narration transcript, the alignment file, and the builder's own notes. Treat the
high-|d| layout and palette metrics as reliable, the type metrics as reliable only when
non-null, and the character and horizon metrics as **no evidence at all**. If the gauntlet
loop needs an automated tiebreak on style, these numbers are not it — a vision-capable
model, or a human, still is.
